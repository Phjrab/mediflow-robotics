# ACT-A V3: grasp occurs, basket placement fails — diagnostic

Date: 2026-10-07 KST. This report is read-only analysis and trace-only instrumentation; it is **not** a successful robot trial or authorization to start another rollout.

Later evidence: a supervised 60-second retry at 18:30 KST saved synchronized image/joint traces and reproduced grasp-without-placement. The new initial gripper (5.45) was typical of training, but elbow was about 21.1 degrees behind the model target when the gripper reopening command advanced. See `trial_1791365427/RESULT.md` and its images/plots. The older hypotheses below are retained as the prior investigation, not a confirmed final root cause.

## What is established

- The A-v3 trim05 training dataset has 60 episodes. In five sampled ceiling-camera episodes (0, 1, 15, 30, 59), the A-large bottle starts outside the right A basket and ends inside it. Thus complete placement demonstrations are present in the training data. This does not establish that all 60 episodes have equally good labels or trajectories.
- LeLab 8022 runs the ACT policy from three camera frames plus joint state; the separate MediFlow VLM adapter is not in this action loop. Lack of VLM integration alone does **not** explain failure to execute a demonstrated fixed-layout placement.
- The 2026-10-07 13:33 ACT-A V3 rollout log is `/home/USER/.cache/huggingface/lerobot/inference_logs/1791347575.log`, with 1,348 joint-trace samples in its sibling `.joints.jsonl`. The arm returned near its starting joint pose by about 20–25 s and then remained near that pose through the recorded 46.77 s. The log shows signal 15 at 13:34:12; the source of that stop signal is not established. The rollout did not reach its configured 60-second duration, but the long near-home interval makes more time alone an unlikely fix.
- The previous claim that the 44° elbow difference at 10 s was **motor tracking error** was too strong. The old trace compared `policy_target_pre_clamp` with `measured`, not the actual command sent after the 5° relative-target clamp. At 10 s those values were approximately -49° and -5°; the actual command could only be within roughly 5° of the then-current motor position. During the first 20 s, the pre-clamp target exceeded the 5° bound on 400/567 elbow samples, 233/567 shoulder-lift samples, and 187/567 gripper samples. Repeated clipping can desynchronize the demonstrated grasp/transfer/release sequence, but the trace alone cannot quantify actual servo lag.
- The rollout started with gripper state 29.56. In the 60 trimmed A demonstrations, 50 episode starts were below 10 and 59 were below 20; only one started above 20. This is an unusual state for the trained distribution, even though the prior gate's broad [0,30] gripper envelope allowed it.
- Offline first-chunk sensitivity check held three previously captured scene images and five body-joint values fixed, changing only gripper input from 5.0 to 29.559. The model's first gripper target changed from about 3.35 to 16.39, a 13.04-unit difference. The images were from an earlier scene capture, not the exact 13:33 live frames, so this demonstrates model sensitivity, not proof of the physical failure's sole cause. Outputs are in `gripper_state_sensitivity_20261007/metrics.json` and `chunks.npz`.

## Current interpretation

The dataset contains complete placement examples, but the 13:33 run did not replicate the corresponding physical sequence. The strongest concrete discrepancies are an unusual gripper start state and frequent 5° target clipping during the active motion phase. It is not yet possible to say whether the bottle was held until the intended drop, released early, or never fully secured; no synchronized rollout video was recorded. More training steps or a longer runtime should not be presented as a confirmed remedy.
